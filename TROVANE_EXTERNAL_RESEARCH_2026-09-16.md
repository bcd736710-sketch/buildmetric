## Repeated external themes and search intents

The repeated signals were extracted as questions and intents only; no competitor structure or prose was copied.

1. Fit and sizing: usable interior dimensions, pet posture, entry size, and loaded shape matter more than a weight label alone.
2. Safety and claim substantiation: restraint architecture, exact configuration, instructions, and evidence must match any crash or safety wording.
3. Hydration away from home: buyers care about carry format, clean water access, drinking interface, cleaning, leakage, and trip duration.
4. Containment and hygiene: portable litter products are judged across setup, wall stability, litter scatter, leakage, emptying, washing, drying, odor, and refolding.
5. Loaded-use testing: empty product photos do not answer structure, strap comfort, movement, stability, or recovery after folding.
6. Durability without unsupported claims: buyers look for repeat operation, fold cycles, hardware/stitching consistency, and defined stop rules, but observations are not lifetime or safety certifications.
7. Travel journey planning: restraint/containment, hydration, hygiene, identification, rest stops, destination rules, and cleanup form one customer job.
8. Retailer considerations: clear intended use, accurate dimensions, care instructions, compatibility limits, packaging, and expectation management help reduce avoidable returns.
9. Wholesale/private label: stock versus light customization versus ODM, exact sample approval, material documentation, logo/color/packaging decisions, MOQ, and lead-time confirmation are recurring procurement intents.

## Content-gap analysis

### Opportunity 1

Topic: Vehicle Pet Tether Evidence & Retail Claim Audit: What Buyers Must Verify Before Listing  
Related Product: Dual-Use Pet Car Seat Tether  
External Evidence: The Center for Pet Safety explicitly advises against extension tethers and says they can negate harness crash-protection claims. VCA and Chewy provide broader signals that in-car restraint and clear travel instructions matter.  
Existing TROVANE Coverage: The existing tether guide already covers attachment, harness connection, adjustment, movement, hardware, stitching, sample checks, and warns against unsupported crash-tested/certified claims. The product page avoids a crash-test claim but presents the item as a car-travel positioning product.  
Content Gap: TROVANE has not addressed the direct external advisory, the difference between movement-management positioning and collision-protection evidence, or a documented claim-approval gate. This is a material gap, but it is primarily a product governance and evidence gap rather than an ordinary SEO article gap.  
B2B Intent: Very high. Retail buyers need to decide whether they can list the product, what instructions and warnings are required, what evidence supports claims, and whether the exact architecture is acceptable in the destination market.  
Score: 96/100

Decision: HOLD — PRODUCT/EVIDENCE REVIEW REQUIRED. Do not commission a new SEO article until TROVANE has reviewed the exact product architecture, intended-use statement, instructions, testing/evidence, destination-market requirements, and current use of "safety" language. Prefer correcting the product page and existing tether guide first if the review finds a mismatch.

## Final content decision

No candidate clears the publishability gate for a new article today.

- Opportunity 1 has the strongest signal and highest business importance, but it is blocked by a safety/evidence review that must precede SEO publication.
- Opportunities 2 and 3 are useful asset ideas, but TROVANE already covers their editorial search intents in depth.
- Hiking hydration, travel checklists, retractable-leash selection, leak testing, and general pet-travel assortment planning were also screened out because they are already covered or are too broad/consumer-oriented relative to B2B procurement conversion.
